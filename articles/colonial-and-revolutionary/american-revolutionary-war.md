review:
  - question: Why did the American victory at Saratoga matter far beyond the battlefield?
    answer: It was the first surrender of a British army to another nation, and the news persuaded France's foreign minister to offer a formal alliance; the treaties signed in February 1778 brought French money, troops and ships and turned the rebellion into a global war.
  - question: Why did most Native nations side with Britain, and how did the war turn out for them?
    answer: Nations such as the Shawnee, Creek, Cherokee and most of the Six Nations hoped a British victory would hold back settlers west of the Appalachians; American victory opened those lands to expansion, and even the Oneida, who fought for the Americans, lost villages in the war and saw promises to protect their lands go unenforced.
  - question: How did the war open paths to freedom for enslaved people, and why were those paths limited?
    answer: British offers of freedom such as Dunmore's 1775 proclamation led tens of thousands to flee their enslavers, and some Black men won freedom by serving in American units; but many who reached British lines were still held by loyalists, and the evacuations of 1782–1783 carried some to freedom in Nova Scotia and others onward in slavery.
  - question: Why did the war not end immediately after Yorktown?
    answer: Britain still held New York, Charleston and other posts with about 26,000 troops, frontier warfare continued, and peace required a change of government in London, preliminary articles in November 1782 and a final treaty in September 1783.

# American Revolutionary War

The American Revolutionary War, also called the War of Independence, was the armed conflict of 1775–1783 between Great Britain and thirteen of its North American colonies. It began as a clash between Massachusetts militia and British regulars, grew into a war for independence after 1776, and became a global war once France, Spain and the Dutch Republic joined against Britain.[^s1][^s5] The Treaty of Paris, signed on September 3, 1783, ended it with British recognition of the United States.[^s8] The Department of Veterans Affairs estimates that about 217,000 Americans served, within a range of 184,000 to 250,000, and counts 4,435 battle deaths and 6,188 wounded on the American side.[^s13]

## Background

After the Seven Years' War left Britain deeply in debt, Parliament barred settlement west of the Appalachians, taxed the colonists directly for the first time with the Stamp Act of 1765, and sent regiments to Boston in 1768. The Coercive Acts of 1774, which colonists called the Intolerable Acts, put the government of Massachusetts under direct British control.[^s1] Colonial leaders there began stockpiling arms and powder.[^s2] General Thomas Gage, the British commander in Boston, sent regulars on a secret march to destroy the stores at Concord, eighteen miles away.[^s2]

## What happened

**War in the North, 1775–1777.** On April 19, 1775, militia met the regulars on Lexington Green before dawn and again at Concord's North Bridge; more than 4,000 militiamen then harried the column along sixteen miles of road back to Boston. The British lost 273 killed, wounded or missing and the colonists 95, and the siege of Boston began the next day.[^s2] On June 14 the Continental Congress voted to raise a Continental Army, and on June 15 it unanimously chose George Washington of Virginia to command it.[^s3] The British took Breed's Hill in June 1775 only at heavy cost, then drove Washington from New York in 1776; his surprise attack on the Hessians at Trenton on December 25, 1776, kept the army alive.[^s1] In the winter of 1777–1778 more than 2,500 soldiers died of disease and exposure at Valley Forge.[^s1]

**Saratoga and the alliance.** On October 17, 1777, John Burgoyne surrendered an army of more than 6,000 to Horatio Gates at Saratoga, New York, the first time a British army had surrendered to another nation.[^s4] The news led France's foreign minister, the Comte de Vergennes, to offer an alliance; Benjamin Franklin, Silas Deane and Arthur Lee signed the Treaty of Alliance and the Treaty of Amity and Commerce on February 6, 1778, and Spain entered the war on June 21, 1779.[^s5] France sent supplies, money, soldiers and a navy.[^s5]

**The southern war and Yorktown.** Britain then turned south, capturing cities in Georgia and South Carolina, including Charleston, but lacked the troops to hold the interior, where fighting between patriots and loyalists became a bitter civil war.[^s1][^s11] In 1781 Washington moved his army to Virginia together with French forces, including the army of the Comte de Rochambeau that had landed at Newport, Rhode Island, in 1780.[^s1][^s6] After de Grasse's French fleet beat off a British fleet at the Battle of the Capes on September 5, about 17,600 allied soldiers besieged roughly 8,300 British troops at Yorktown; French and American assault parties took Redoubts 9 and 10 on October 14, and Cornwallis's army laid down its arms on October 19.[^s6]

## The war's many sides

The war was fought by far more than the Continental Army. Women ran farms and businesses while men were away, and some faced raids on their own homes.[^s1] Loyalists fought for the Crown, and about 60,000 left the country by the war's end.[^s1]

For enslaved people the war offered dangerous chances at freedom. In 1775 Virginia's royal governor, Lord Dunmore, promised freedom to people enslaved by patriots who fled to fight for the king.[^s9] Only 500 to 1,000 joined his regiment, but historians estimate that 30,000 to 100,000 enslaved people escaped their enslavers during the war.[^s1] Washington at first barred Black men from the Continental Army, then reversed himself as manpower ran short; in 1778 the 1st Rhode Island Regiment enlisted enslaved men with a promise of freedom, and 140 of its 225 soldiers were Black.[^s9] Freedom won behind British lines was fragile: when the British left Charleston in December 1782, more than 5,000 people of African descent sailed with them, most of them still enslaved by loyalists, while about 3,000 Black loyalists were resettled in Nova Scotia in 1783.[^s11][^s9]

Native nations made their own choices. The Shawnee, Creek, Cherokee and most of the Haudenosaunee (Six Nations) sided with Britain, hoping to stop settlers from moving west of the Appalachians.[^s1][^s10] The Oneida and Tuscarora sided with the Americans: Oneida warriors fought at Oriskany in August 1777, and about fifty Oneida men served at Valley Forge. The Clinton-Sullivan expedition of 1779 struck the British-allied Six Nations, and in 1780 one of the main Oneida villages was itself destroyed.[^s10]

## Aftermath and legacy

Yorktown did not end the fighting. Britain still had about 26,000 troops in North America,[^s6] and on August 19, 1782, about 50 British rangers and 300 Native warriors routed Kentucky militia at Blue Licks, killing about 64.[^s12] In London, Parliament authorized peace talks in February 1782 and Lord North's government fell in March.[^s7] Franklin, John Adams and John Jay signed preliminary articles on November 30, 1782, and the final treaty followed on September 3, 1783; its terms recognized independence and boundaries, settled fishing rights off Newfoundland, and addressed prewar debts to British creditors, the property of loyalists and the withdrawal of British forces.[^s8] The last British troops left New York City on November 25, 1783.[^s7]

Independence opened western lands to American expansion and, as one textbook puts it, marked the beginning of the end of Native independence.[^s1] Even allies suffered: the 1784 Treaty of Fort Stanwix promised the Oneida and Tuscarora their lands, but the government never enforced it, and many Oneida later moved to Wisconsin and Ontario.[^s10]

## Connections

- **George Washington** led the Continental Army from his appointment by Congress in June 1775.[^s3]
- The war was fought across the **Thirteen Colonies**, from Massachusetts to Georgia.[^s1]
- It led to the **Treaty of Paris (1783)**, in which Britain recognized American independence.[^s8]
- It split and devastated the **Haudenosaunee** Confederacy, whose nations fought on both sides.[^s10]
- It reshaped the lives of **African Americans**, as enslaved people fled to the British, Black soldiers served in American units, and Black loyalists left with the British.[^s9]
- Related topics include the **Declaration of Independence** of 1776, the **Second Continental Congress**, the **Treaty of Alliance (1778)** with France, **Dunmore's Proclamation** and the **Articles of Confederation** that governed the states during the war.[^s1][^s5]
