# Wright Company School of Aviation

The Wright Company School of Aviation was the flight school that Orville and Wilbur Wright ran through their airplane company from 1910 to 1916.[^s1][^s4] After a short first season in Montgomery, Alabama, it settled at Huffman Prairie, the pasture east of Dayton, Ohio, where the brothers had turned their 1903 machine into a practical airplane.[^s1][^s3] Between May 1910 and February 1916 the school trained 119 students at Huffman Field, among them several of the first pilots of the U.S. Army.[^s1]

## History

By 1910 the brothers were building airplanes through the new Wright Company, which they had founded with New York financiers; its factory, exhibition team and flying school together brought national attention to Dayton.[^s7] The school and the exhibition team trained and supplied the pilots who would demonstrate and sell the Wrights' machines.[^s1][^s2]

Because Dayton winters were poor for flying, Wilbur went south in February 1910 to find a training site. On February 16 he chose a plantation outside Montgomery, whose owner, Frank Kohn, offered the land free for three months, while the city's Commercial Club cleared it and built a hangar.[^s3] Orville arrived on March 24 and made Montgomery's first powered flight two days later. Engine failures and bad weather kept interrupting lessons, and only one student, Walter Brookins, soloed, on May 6. Brookins also made what the Encyclopedia of Alabama calls the first recorded night flights, on May 25.[^s3] Wilbur had already told Orville to close the camp so the team could prepare for summer exhibitions, and by the end of May the airplane had been shipped north.[^s3] The Alabama site later became Maxwell Field, today Maxwell Air Force Base.[^s3][^s4]

That spring the school moved its permanent base to Huffman Field, reached from downtown Dayton by an interurban car that ran every thirty minutes to Simms Station.[^s1][^s2] Orville was the principal instructor, assisted by pilots he had trained; Arthur L. Welsh, described in the Wright State University archives as the first person to learn to fly at the school, became one of them.[^s1][^s6] A wooden hangar served both the school and the company's test flights of new models.[^s1] The field's size and uneven ground forced students to fly a set course with only a few places to take off.[^s2] The school advertised that it turned out competent pilots eight or ten days after a student's first flight.[^s1] It also ran branches elsewhere: the U.S. Centennial of Flight Commission's chronology records a Wright school opening at Augusta, Georgia, in January 1911 and another at Belmont Park, New York, in June 1911, with Welsh as instructor.[^s5]

In May 1911 the Army sent Lieutenants Henry H. "Hap" Arnold and Thomas DeWitt Milling to Dayton, along with Lt. John Rodgers, for lessons.[^s4][^s5] Arnold and Milling went on to help start the Army's first permanent flight school at College Park, Maryland.[^s8] Civilian students included Calbraith Perry Rodgers, Griffith Brewer and several women, among them Marjorie Stinson.[^s1][^s4] Flying remained dangerous: Welsh was killed in a crash at College Park on June 11, 1912.[^s6] Orville sold the Wright Company in October 1915, and about a year later the school at Huffman Field closed.[^s1][^s7]

## Role

The school was a business arm as well as a classroom. It shared a field and hangar with the company's test flying and with the Wright exhibition team, which drew paying crowds from 1910 to 1911, and both helped market the Wrights' airplanes to civilian and military buyers.[^s1][^s2] For the Army, a private company school trained officers who then carried flight training into the Army's own first permanent school.[^s1][^s8] Arnold, who learned there, commanded the U.S. Army Air Corps during World War II.[^s1] In 1917 the Army leased the land around the field for Wilbur Wright Field, a World War I school for pilots, armorers and mechanics, and the 1910 hangar was torn down in 1938.[^s1] Today the field is part of Dayton Aviation Heritage National Historical Park, where the school is remembered as one of the ways Dayton became a center of early aviation.[^s2][^s7]

## Connections

- The school was **located in** [Huffman Prairie Flying Field](us:huffman-prairie), which it used from 1910 to 1916 for lessons and company test flights.[^s1]
- It was **part of** the Wright Company, which coordinated the school and the exhibition team at Huffman Field.[^s2]
- It was **founded by** Orville and Wilbur Wright and **led by** Orville, its principal instructor.[^s1][^s4]
- It **requires** the [Wright brothers' first flight](us:wright-brothers-first-flight) as background, and is related to the Montgomery site that became Maxwell Air Force Base, the College Park flight school its Army students helped start, and Wilbur Wright Field.[^s3][^s8][^s1]
