  - question: Why did the Texas Revolution break out in 1835, and what part did slavery play?

Slavery mattered too, since Americans had kept using enslaved labor despite Mexico's 1829 ban, and the Republic's 1836 constitution then legalized slavery and barred free Black people without Congress's consent.
  - question: How did the annexation of Texas lead to war with Mexico?
    answer: Mexico had never accepted Texas independence and threatened war over annexation. Texas claimed the Rio Grande as its border while Mexico said the line was the Nueces River; when Polk sent Zachary Taylor's army into the land between the rivers and skirmishes followed, Congress declared war on May 13, 1846, a war that ended with Mexico ceding about 525,000 square miles in 1848.
  - question: Why did Texas secede in 1861, and how did the Supreme Court later treat that secession?
    answer: The convention's Declaration of Causes said Texas had entered the Union as a slaveholding state and treated Northern opposition to slavery as a threat that justified leaving. After the war, the Court in Texas v. White held that the Constitution created an indestructible Union and that the secession ordinance was legally null, so Texas had never ceased to be a state.

# Texas

Texas is a U.S. state in the south-central part of the country that has lived under Native, Spanish, Mexican, independent, Confederate and U.S. rule. After declaring independence from Mexico on March 2, 1836, it existed as a separate republic until President James K. Polk signed the act making it the 28th state on December 29, 1845.[^s1] The 2020 census counted 29,145,505 Texans; the Census Bureau estimated 31,709,821 residents in 2025.[^s9]

## Geography

Texas covered 261,267.85 square miles of land in 2020, according to the Census Bureau.[^s9] Its rivers shaped its history as much as its size. When Texas joined the Union, Texans claimed the Rio Grande as their southwestern border while Mexico insisted the line was the Nueces River farther north, and that disputed strip became the trigger for war in 1846.[^s12] The Red River to the north became the line across which federal officials pushed the Native peoples of the Brazos reservations in 1859.[^s4] In the southeast, the Gulf Coast around Beaumont and Port Arthur became the heart of the oil industry after 1901.[^s11]

## History

**Native nations and Spanish Texas.** Long before Europeans arrived, Texas was home to many peoples. Caddo communities farmed the eastern woodlands; Spanish soldiers and missionaries reached them in the late 1680s, and a smallpox epidemic in 1690–91 killed about 3,000 Caddos.[^s4] Coahuiltecan bands lived between the San Antonio River and the Rio Grande, the Jumanos worked as traders between peoples by the mid-1600s, and the Comanches, mounted on horses that spread from Spanish settlements, displaced the Apaches from the southern plains and became Spain's most formidable opponents.[^s4]

**Mexican Texas and revolution.** American settlers in the Mexican province of Coahuila and Texas kept using enslaved labor despite Mexico's 1829 ban on slavery; in April 1830 Mexico tried to restrict American settlement.[^s10] When President Antonio López de Santa Anna centralized power and repealed the federal Constitution of 1824, settlers rebelled, firing on Mexican troops at Gonzales in October 1835.[^s10] Tejanos, Texans of Mexican and Indigenous descent, fought alongside them.[^s10] The Alamo fell on March 6, 1836, with all its defenders killed, and about 300 prisoners were executed at Goliad, but Sam Houston's army defeated and captured Santa Anna at San Jacinto.[^s10]

**The Republic.** The Republic's constitution of March 16, 1836, legalized slavery, let immigrants from the United States bring enslaved people, and forbade free Black people to live in Texas without the consent of Congress.[^s3] Under President Mirabeau Lamar, who took office in December 1838, the Republic drove out the Cherokees and forced Shawnees, Alabamas and Coushattas from their lands; the 1840 Council House Fight in San Antonio set off years of war with the Comanches.[^s3] The Kickapoos, Delawares and Caddos were expelled as well, while the Alabama-Coushatta escaped removal.[^s4] By annexation the Republic's debt had reached about $12 million.[^s3]

**Annexation and war.** The U.S. Senate rejected an annexation treaty in June 1844, so Congress used a joint resolution, which Tyler signed on March 1, 1845.[^s1] Texas kept its public lands and its debt, and could divide into as many as four more states if it chose.[^s2] Mexico had threatened war over annexation; after Polk sent Zachary Taylor's army into the disputed land and fighting broke out, the United States declared war on May 13, 1846, and in 1848 Mexico ceded about 525,000 square miles.[^s12]

**Secession, emancipation and removal.** On February 1, 1861, a convention in Austin adopted an Ordinance of Secession over Sam Houston's protest that it had not been put to a popular vote, and the Confederacy accepted Texas on March 1.[^s6] The convention's Declaration of Causes was explicit that the state had joined the Union "holding, maintaining and protecting the institution known as negro slavery," and it defended slavery as the reason to leave.[^s5] On June 19, 1865, Maj. Gen. Gordon Granger announced at Galveston that "all slaves are free"; the order's closing lines, telling freedpeople to keep working for their former enslavers for wages, foreshadowed the long fight for equal rights that followed, and the date became Juneteenth.[^s8] On the plains, after the second Battle of Adobe Walls in June 1874, U.S. troops attacked Native camps in Palo Duro Canyon that September; most Comanches surrendered by winter and the last small bands came in by 1875.[^s4]

## Today

The Spindletop gusher of January 10, 1901, which flowed an estimated 100,000 barrels a day, gave birth to the modern petroleum industry and to companies such as the Texas Company (later Texaco), Gulf Oil and Humble (later Exxon).[^s11] Texas grew by about 2.6 million people between the 2020 census and the Census Bureau's 2025 estimate, and 17.6 percent of residents were born abroad in the bureau's 2020–2024 survey data.[^s9] The Caddo Nation now lives mainly in western Oklahoma near Binger, while the Alabama-Coushatta, the Tigua of El Paso and the Kickapoo keep reservations within Texas.[^s13][^s4]
