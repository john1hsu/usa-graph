# California

California is the thirty-first state of the United States, admitted on September 9, 1850, as a free state under the Compromise of 1850.[^s1] Long before that it was the homeland of many Native Californian peoples, then a Spanish colony from 1769 and a Mexican province from 1821, until the United States took it in the U.S.–Mexican War.[^s6][^s7][^s4] It has been the most populous state since 1962.[^s8]

## Geography

California lies on the boundary between two tectonic plates: along the San Andreas fault system, more than 800 miles long, the Pacific Plate moves northwest past the North American Plate by as much as 2 inches a year, according to the U.S. Geological Survey.[^s15] Between the Sierra Nevada on the east and the Coast Ranges on the west lies the Central Valley, about 400 miles long and 20,000 square miles in area, drained by the Sacramento and San Joaquin Rivers.[^s14] The state also holds Badwater Basin in Death Valley, at 282 feet below sea level the lowest point in North America.[^s11]

## History

**Native California and Spanish colonization.** Before Europeans arrived, the region was home to dozens of Native peoples; twentieth-century anthropologists identified 67 tribes, and the Native population before the American takeover is estimated at about 310,000.[^s9][^s13] Spanish explorers named the tip of Baja California "California" in 1533, and Juan Rodríguez Cabrillo sailed the coast in 1542.[^s6] Colonization began in 1769, when an expedition led by Gaspar de Portolá and Junípero Serra founded outposts at San Diego and Monterey; Serra founded Mission San Diego de Alcalá on July 16, 1769, in the homeland of the Kumeyaay, and a Native uprising burned its church in 1775.[^s10] By 1823 the Franciscans had founded 21 missions from San Diego to Sonoma, which Spain used to convert Native people, secure its frontier and obtain their labor.[^s6] A historian writing for the state's Native American Heritage Commission estimates that about 100,000 Native people died as a direct result of the missions, many from introduced disease.[^s9]

**Mexican California.** After Mexico won independence in 1821, power shifted from missionaries to secular governors and ranching families.[^s7] Secularization of the missions began in 1834; half of the mission lands were meant for Native communities, but distribution was haphazard, and Mexico issued more than 500 land grants, many to Californio families such as the Vallejos, Peraltas and Picos.[^s7] Native Californians did most of the work on these ranchos, often bound in peonage, while ranchers traded hides and tallow with American ships.[^s7]

**Conquest and statehood.** War between the United States and Mexico began in 1846, the year of the Bear Flag Revolt in California.[^s7][^s1] The Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo, signed February 2, 1848, transferred California and other lands to the United States; it made Mexicans who stayed U.S. citizens unless they chose otherwise and promised to protect their property, though the Senate struck the article protecting Mexican land grants.[^s4] Gold was found on January 24, 1848, and more than 300,000 people arrived by 1850.[^s1][^s5] In 1849, 48 delegates met in Monterey and wrote a constitution, published in English and Spanish, that limited voting to white men.[^s12] Congress admitted California the next year after heated debate over slavery.[^s1] The Californio Peralta family later lost all but 700 of its 49,000 East Bay acres to lawyers, taxes, squatters and speculators.[^s5]

**Removal, forced labor and exclusion.** The first legislature's Act for the Government and Protection of Indians (1850) barred Native people from testifying in court and let whites and Californios hold them as indentured servants.[^s5] State-organized militias such as the Mariposa Battalion campaigned against Native communities, and in 1852 the U.S. Senate refused to ratify 18 treaties that had promised California tribes about 7.5 million acres.[^s9] The Native population fell from about 150,000 in 1848 to about 30,000 twelve years later, through disease and violence by miners.[^s5][^s13] A $20-a-month foreign miners' tax fell on Latin American miners, Chinese residents could not testify in court until 1872, and Black Californians, more than 2,000 by 1852, won that right only in 1863.[^s5]

**Twentieth century.** Oil, agriculture and entertainment drew millions to southern California in the 1920s and 1930s, and wartime aerospace and shipbuilding brought millions more, including the first large wave of Black migrants, who faced discriminatory housing and hiring.[^s8] After the attack on Pearl Harbor, the federal government removed about 93,000 Californians of Japanese descent, most held for more than two years without charges.[^s8] In 1966 César Chávez and Dolores Huerta founded the United Farm Workers, and after federal immigration law changed in 1965, California became the main destination for Asian and Pacific immigrants.[^s8]

## Today

The 2020 census counted 39,538,223 Californians, the largest population of any state, although California lost one seat in the House of Representatives in that apportionment (Census Bureau, 2021).[^s2] Its gross domestic product was about $4.10 trillion in 2024, out of a national total of about $29.18 trillion (BEA, 2025).[^s3] The Central Valley alone supplies about a quarter of the nation's food on less than 1 percent of its farmland, according to the U.S. Geological Survey.[^s14] The capital has been Sacramento since 1854.[^s1]
