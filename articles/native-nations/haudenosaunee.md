# Haudenosaunee

The **Haudenosaunee** — "People of the Longhouse," a metaphor for their political alliance — are a confederacy of Native nations long known to outsiders as the Iroquois Confederacy or the Six Nations.[^s4] Its members are the five founding nations, the Mohawk, Oneida, Onondaga, Cayuga and Seneca, and the Tuscarora, who joined in 1722.[^s4][^s2] Each nation speaks its own Iroquoian language, and descent and property traditionally pass through the mother's line.[^s12] The Grand Council still meets at the Onondaga Nation south of Syracuse, New York, and the nations regard their treaties with the United States as binding agreements between sovereigns.[^s2][^s3]

## History

**The Great Law of Peace.** In Haudenosaunee tradition, the warring five nations were brought together by a messenger called the Peacemaker.[^s1] Jikonhsaseh, a woman who had encouraged conflict, accepted peace first, and in return the Peacemaker gave women the duty of Clan Mother; the grieving Hiawatha became his partner; and the Onondaga leader Tadodaho, the last holdout, came to preside over the Grand Council.[^s1] The nations buried their weapons beneath a white pine, the Tree of Peace.[^s1][^s12] The date is uncertain: estimates range from the mid-1500s to August 31, 1142, proposed by Barbara Mann and Jerry Fields, and the Onondaga Nation speaks of more than a thousand years ago.[^s4][^s1]

**Colonial era.** In the fur-trade era the Haudenosaunee destroyed or dispersed neighbouring peoples, including the Huron-Wendat in 1649, and fought repeated wars with New France.[^s4] In 1751 Benjamin Franklin, urging the British colonies to unite, wrote that it would be strange if "six Nations of ignorant Savages" could form such a union while the colonies could not — a line that shows both his awareness of the league and his contempt for its people.[^s7] Haudenosaunee delegates attended the 1754 Albany Congress, where Franklin's Albany Plan of Union was drafted.[^s10]

**Revolution and loss.** The 1768 Boundary Line Treaty at Fort Stanwix pushed the colonial boundary deep into Oneida land.[^s6] In the Revolution the confederacy split: the Oneida and Tuscarora supported the Americans, fighting beside the New York militia at Oriskany on August 6, 1777, while most of the other nations sided with Britain.[^s6][^s4] In 1779 the Clinton-Sullivan expedition burned many Seneca, Onondaga and Cayuga towns, and in 1780 a principal Oneida village was destroyed.[^s4][^s6] After the war many Haudenosaunee followed the Mohawk leader Joseph Brant to a British grant on the Grand River, now the Six Nations of the Grand River reserve in Ontario.[^s4]

**Treaties and dispossession.** The treaties of Fort Stanwix (1784) and Fort Harmar (1789) affirmed that the Haudenosaunee and the United States were separate nations and set boundaries meant to stop settlement.[^s3] The Treaty of Canandaigua, signed November 11, 1794, declared peace and friendship, recognized the lands of the Oneida, Onondaga, Cayuga and Seneca, pledged that the United States would never claim or disturb them, and promised an annual payment in goods.[^s5] Yet the federal government never enforced the 1784 guarantee of Oneida lands, and New York State purchases beginning in 1785 eventually reduced the Oneida homeland in New York to a 32-acre reservation; many Oneida moved to Wisconsin from the 1820s and to Ontario in the 1840s.[^s6] New York took the Onondaga Nation's wampum belts in 1890 and returned them only in 1989.[^s2]

In the twentieth century the Seneca Nation lost land the 1794 treaty had protected. Federal courts upheld taking Allegany Reservation land by eminent domain for the Kinzua Dam, and when it was completed in 1965 it flooded about 10,000 acres of habitable land, relocated about 700 people and some 3,000 graves, and submerged the Pennsylvania grant made to the Seneca leader Cornplanter in 1791.[^s11] A 1964 reparations law of about $15 million also required a plan to end the Senecas' federal relationship.[^s11]

## Government and sovereignty

The Grand Council has 50 chiefs — 9 Mohawk, 9 Oneida, 14 Onondaga, 10 Cayuga and 8 Seneca — each with an equal voice; the Tuscarora are represented through the Cayuga.[^s1][^s4] The chiefs sit on three benches: Mohawk and Seneca as Older Brothers, Oneida and Cayuga as Younger Brothers, and Onondaga as Firekeepers. Each bench must agree before passing a matter on, and a decision is made only when all three reach "One Mind."[^s1] Clan Mothers counsel their clans and select chiefs, whose titles are hereditary within a mother's lineage.[^s1][^s4] Onondaga is the Central Fire and the confederacy's capital.[^s2]

The Onondaga Nation still governs itself through its fourteen chiefs and their Clan Mothers, has resisted pressure to adopt an elected tribal council, and does not take part in the U.S. census.[^s2]

## Today

Haudenosaunee people live in their own territories and reservations in New York, Wisconsin and Oklahoma, in Canada, and across North America.[^s12] The largest Canadian communities are Six Nations of the Grand River (29,024 band members, April 2024), Akwesasne, which straddles the border (13,412), and Kahnawake near Montreal (11,715).[^s4] The Onondaga Nation has pressed claims against New York State over lands it says were taken in violation of federal law.[^s2]

## Debates and interpretations

In 1987 the Senate stated that framers such as Washington and Franklin "greatly admired" the Six Nations' principles, and on October 21, 1988, Congress acknowledged the contributions of the Iroquois Confederacy and other Indian nations to the nation's formation.[^s10][^s8] Scholars Donald Grinde and Bruce Johansen argue that colonists saw Native governments as models of liberty, citing Canasatego's 1744 speech urging colonial union and Franklin's 1751 letter.[^s9] West Virginia University's Indigenous Appalachia project likewise describes the confederacy's democracy as an influence on the U.S. government.[^s12] The anthropologist Elisabeth Tooker countered that the framers drew on European theory and precedent, that direct evidence of influence is thin, and that the league's matrilineal clans, chiefs chosen by Clan Mothers, ranked seating of nations and lack of central authority have little in common with the federal Constitution.[^s9]

## Connections

- **Participated in the American Revolutionary War and the Battle of Oriskany.** The confederacy split and suffered heavily; Oneida warriors fought at Oriskany.[^s6][^s4]
- **Affected the Albany Plan.** Haudenosaunee delegates attended the 1754 Albany Congress, and Franklin had cited their union as a precedent for joining the colonies.[^s10][^s7]
- **Treaties and tribal sovereignty.** Fort Stanwix (1784) and Canandaigua (1794) define the nations' relationship with the United States, and the Kinzua Dam flooded Seneca land Canandaigua had protected; Congress's 1988 resolution reaffirmed the government-to-government relationship.[^s3][^s5][^s11][^s8]
